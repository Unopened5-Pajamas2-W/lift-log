## Claims (atomic)

**Recovery time course, failure vs non-failure (Zourdos on Pareja-Blanco et al. 2018, JSCR)**
- 10 trained men (2–4 yrs), 20 wks, 10 conditions; each session = 3 sets Smith bench + squat; fatigue pre, 6h, 24h, 48h. [§Methods]
- Failure protocols: 12(12), 10(10), 8(8), 6(6), 4(4) [reps done(reps possible)]; non-failure: 6(12), 5(10), 4(8), 3(6), 2(4) — proximity range 5RPE–8RPE. Load-% mapping: 12 reps ≈70%, 10 ≈75%, 8 ≈80%, 6 ≈85%, 4 ≈90% 1RM. [§Methods]
- Every failure protocol dropped vertical jump at 24h (−4.4% to −7.2%); zero non-failure protocols did. At 48h, failure protocols with ≥6 reps still ~−4% VJ; non-failure <−0.7%. [§Findings]
- CK elevated at 48h in all failure conditions; highest in 12(12)/10(10)/8(8). [§Findings]
- Volume-equation example: 5(10) vs 4(4) ≈ equal tonnage (1,050 vs 1,080 kg @100-kg squatter) yet failure still lengthened recovery → failure's fatigue cost is not just volume. [§Interpretation]
- Watkins 2017 anchor: 8% VJ drop predicted 28% drop in squat reps @80% 1RM (r=0.65) — >2 lost reps/set. [§Interpretation]
- Programming rule: "12 sets/week squat over 3 sessions" — 5 sets to failure @70% (15,10,7,6,5 reps) blocks retraining until ~Friday and breaks 3× frequency; 4×8 at RPE 5–8 (2–5 RIR) permits Wed/Fri sessions. Weekly volume/frequency beats per-session volume. [§Application]
- Proximity guidance: stay further from failure in volume/accumulation blocks; closer to failure in intensity/peaking blocks. Don't train habitually at 5RPE — leaves strength on the table (Helms dissertation: 5–6 RPE 3×/wk × 8 wks gave similar hypertrophy to ~8 RPE in well-trained lifters). [§Application]
- Failure acceptable for: single-joint assistance work; last session before longest break (e.g., Friday before 72h rest); only final set of a main lift; keep technique tight at 10RPE (breakdown is a known cost). Failure ≠ more strength (Davies 2016 meta). [§Application]

**Individualized RFD response: strength vs power (Zourdos on Peltonen et al. 2018, EJAP)**
- 24 untrained men; 14 trained 2×/wk × 20 wks (leg press/extension/curl); wks 1–10 strength block: 2–4 sets × 6–10 reps @ 70–80% 1RM; wks 11–20 power block: 3–5 sets × 4–10 reps @ 30–50% 1RM. Isometric leg-extension RFD pre/post every 7th session; 10 controls. [§Methods]
- Responders: 6 max-strength responders (+100% RFD strength block, +3% after power block), 4 power responders (+11% strength block, +53% power block), 4 non-responders. Hypertrophy +12% in both responder types, ~0 in non-responders; leg-press 1RM gains similar across groups (+12.38%/+15.17%/+16.11%). [§Findings]
- Power responder heuristic: 2:1 ratio of normal strength work (≥70% 1RM, multiple reps, ≥5 RPE) to power work; in 3×/wk DUP make mid-week session power-focused; in block periodization 1 power block per 3. [§Application]
- Alternative power formats: dynamic effort 40–60% 1RM × 2–3 reps/set; or high-intensity singles @ 80–90% 1RM; stop sets before bar velocity slows. [§Application]
- Under-recovery signal: consistently short of failure, only 2–5 sets/exercise, high frequency, yet chronically under-recovered → swap one session for power work; power sessions double as taper/deload. [§Application]
- Non-responders: prioritize frequent-ish heavy singles on main lifts at 7–8 RPE every few weeks (possibly weekly), low volume/high intensity, neural emphasis. [§Application]
- Context: Hubal 2005 — 0–250% strength-gain range in 12 wks. [§Interpretation]

## Evidence quality
Strong for niche: each article dissects one primary study + 5–9 supporting refs. Notable: Davies 2016 (Sports Med failure meta); Morán-Navarro 2017 (EJAP); Helms 2018 (Front Physiol); Pareja-Blanco 2018 (JSCR); Peltonen 2018 (EJAP); Hubal 2005; Schoenfeld 2016 (rest). Honest limits: n=10–14, untrained-to-intermediate, non-failure conditions confounded by differing rep counts, no velocity monitoring.

## Caveats / superseded-looking
1. Failure gives no extra strength — direction holds, later metas refine (proximity matters little for strength volume-equated). 2. "5–6 RPE ≈ 8 RPE hypertrophy" (dissertation) — predates dose-response work; treat as lower bound, not prescription. 3. Responder typologies on tiny samples — directional, not diagnostic. 4. Power block 30–50% 1RM atypical; article itself prefers velocity-anchored formats. 5. VJ as readiness proxy — one 2017 study; velocity tools preferred since. 6. CK/hormonal markers as fatigue indicators — now considered weak, non-specific proxies.

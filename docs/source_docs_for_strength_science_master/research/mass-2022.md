## Claims (atomic)

**1. Flexible training templates (Zourdos on Walts et al. 2021)**
- Study: 32 DIII lacrosse players (15M/17F), 8 wks (two 4-wk blocks), 3 sessions/wk, 22 sessions; flexible group picked daily from 4 session types (green: high-volume or high-intensity; yellow: low-volume or low-intensity; red = skip) vs fixed order. ~60-min sessions + 2×/wk 60-min speed/agility. Result: no between-group differences (VJ +3.9% flex vs +6.4% fixed; agility +0.8% vs +1.6%). [§Findings]
- Prior evidence: Colquhoun 2017 (trained lifters, 9 wks, fixed hypertrophy/power/strength order, last sets to failure on hyp+strength days): equal strength but flexible group had lower dropout (12.5% vs 31%) and fewer missed sessions (4 vs 8). McNamara & Stearne 2010 (12 wks, 2×/wk, all sets to failure, 20/15/10RM rotation vs choose-any-order; each type 8×): leg press gains ~3× in flexible group; only 1 of 7 strength tests across 3 studies favored flexibility. [§Current and Previous Research]
- Decision rules: use flexible templates only when (1) training extremely demanding, (2) life schedule exhausting, (3) travel/inconsistent gym access. If chronically fatigued, flexibility is not the fix — cut volume or proximity to failure / rearrange the week. [§Flexible Situations]
- Default weekly order (3×/wk muscle frequency): moderate-RPE Mon, low-RPE Wed, high-RPE Fri; or hypertrophy/high-volume Mon → power/low-volume Wed → strength/moderate Fri (power session in middle = 48-h priming + more strength-day volume). [§Flexible Situations]
- Within-month flexibility: 6 high + 6 moderate + 6 low RPE days/month in any order; under stress scale to 4 high / 8 moderate / 12 low (24 sessions). [§Degrees of Flexibility]
- Preferred model: fixed weekly order + permanent "flex option" slot (low-volume power day or low-RPE assistance-only day) to insert when readiness is low; then resume. Miss 1 day → push everything back one day; miss 2 days → repeat the week. [§Application]
- Readiness metrics: HRV failed to improve strength gains or track recovery; well-being scales unproven for acute strength. Best-supported: vertical jump (Watkins 2017: 4 squat sets to failure @80% 1RM, retest 48h — VJ drop correlated with fewer reps, r=0.65); use a cutoff (e.g., 1.5 cm drop) → pick easier session. PRS 0–10: a "7" vs "8–10" day probably doesn't need session change — use RPE/velocity intra-session. Truly trashed → day off or light/power day. [§Determining Readiness]
- Asides: exercise-pool choice ("back assistance: your choice") may aid strength (Rauch 2020); stretches >100 s acutely cut force (Fowles 2000), <10 s risk-free. [§Asides]

**2. Proximity to failure & velocity loss (Zourdos on Andersen et al. 2021 + 13-study table)**
- Study: 10 trained subjects (7F/3M), unilateral leg press + leg extension 2×/wk × 9 wks, within-subject: one leg stopped at 15% VL, other at 30%. Wk-1 loads: leg press 80% 1RM, extension 75% 1RM; target 5–7 reps (15% leg) vs 12–14 reps (30% leg); +2.5–5 kg next set if reps exceed range top; 2.5-min rests; volume equated (double sets for low-VL leg; start-leg rotates weekly). Actual VL 20.2±6.2% vs 36.3±3.3%; estimated RIR ~5–10 vs ~1–4. [§Methods]
- Result: no differences in strength or muscle-thickness gains; RFD, velocity at same %1RM, pennation, fascicle length unchanged; power output decreased at all loads in both groups. (RF ES 0.20 favoring 15% leg, p=0.421 — inconclusive; equivalence testing should have been used.) [§Findings]
- Strength rules: at 50–85% 1RM, a 15–30% VL window (~3–5+ RIR) beats ≥40% VL (≈ failure-ish) for 1RM: 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% VL groups each out-gained ≥40% VL groups. Cannot extend to >85% 1RM: a single @90% 1RM is already ~2 RIR for most → reserve 0–2 RIR for high-intensity, low-volume work; keep volume work farther from failure. Peak intensity matters more than average intensity for strength. [§Interpretation]
- Hypertrophy rules: ≥5 RIR appears sufficient — 4 of 5 VL hypertrophy studies show no close-vs-far difference (one favored close: Martinez-Canton). Caveats: findings exercise-specific (squat/bench may not transfer to curls); synergist effects unknown; short-ROM assistance fatigue doesn't persist; don't avoid failure always — integrate it selectively (failure work + non-failure volume, chosen carefully). [§Interpretation]
- Key numbers: Galiano 2020: squat 1RM +10.7% training at 10% VL @50% 1RM (>15 RIR); huge individual variability (Rodriguez-Rosell ranges: +6.4–58.6% at 10% VL, +4.5–66.2% at 30%, +1.8–52.1% at 40%); Klemp 2016 squat 1RM −3.5% to +18.6% (mean +9.4±5.3%). Andersen study averaged ~40% strength gain. 12 of 13 VL studies NOT volume-equated; 40%-VL groups often did ~double the reps per set at same %1RM — confounding "failure is worse" claims. [§All Outcome Measures/critique]
- Counterexample: Kilgallon 2021 — 3 wks, floor press @85% 1RM: failure beat 20% VL; failure group did 2.8–3.3 reps/set vs 1.1–1.3 (≈2.5× more reps = practice effect), non-maximal intent velocity prescribed, short ROM. Close-to-failure may matter more for short-ROM movements. [§Observations]
- VL mechanics: %VL is exercise- and set-dependent; first-rep velocity drifts across sets (0.70→0.60 m/s @70% 1RM), so same %VL lands at different RIR. Velocity at a given RIR is stable across loads — better method: one set to failure ~75% 1RM to calibrate RIR-velocity relationship, then stop sets at an absolute target velocity rather than %VL. [§Interpretation]
- Explosive adaptations (jump/RFD): keep sets at ≤20% VL. [§Application]

**3. Attentional focus (Nuckols on Grgic et al. 2021 meta)**
- External focus (outcome/effect of movement) vs internal (body parts/muscles). Acute strength: external wins SMD 0.34, p<0.001 (7 studies), incl. big lifts (IMTP, squat/DL). Longitudinal strength: external favored SMD 0.32 (p=0.11) overall; lower-body sub-analysis SMD 0.47 (p=0.02) — only 3 longitudinal studies, tentative. Practical: cue externally when strength is the goal; internal focus may aid hypertrophy per prior MASS coverage. [§article]

**4. Concurrent training (Nuckols on Schumann et al. 2021 meta)**
- 43 studies, 1,090 subjects, ≥4 wks, identical RT ± added aerobic work. Max strength SMD −0.06 (p=0.45, nil); hypertrophy SMD −0.01 (p=0.92, nil); explosive strength SMD −0.28 (p=0.007, real small negative). [§article]
- Subanalyses: modality, endurance frequency, training status, age, same-session vs separate — none modified the strength effect; total endurance duration not analyzed. [§article]
- Decision rules: interference is niche concern, not default. Careful only when: (1) recovery compromised (poor sleep, high stress, calorie deficit); (2) endurance training near recovery limit; (3) lifting volume already near limit + lots of endurance added; (4) goals are explosive/power (then limit endurance). Otherwise ~80% of people can combine both. Separate modalities when possible (separating by 24 h minimizes interference); heavy endurance volume still implicitly crowds out recovery budget. [§article]
- History arc: Hickson 1980 (large) → Wilson 2012 (moderate) → Petré 2021 (none untrained; none trained split) → Schumann 2021 (nil strength/hypertrophy). [§article]

## Evidence quality
Strong and current for 2022 vantage: named peer-reviewed studies, mostly JSCR, Sports Medicine, Scand J Med Sci Sports, 2016–2021. Notable: Andersen 2021 (JSCR); Schumann 2021 (Sports Med, 43 studies/1,090); Grgic 2021 (attentional focus); Pareja-Blanco/Rodríguez-Rosell Spanish VL series (~10 Smith-machine squat trials). Weak spots in-text: one meta a pre-print; longitudinal attentional-focus base only 3 studies; VL literature 12/13 not volume-equated, dominated by one lab's Smith-machine squat.

## Unique content
1. VL→RIR conversions (15% VL ≈ 15.3 RIR @50% 1RM per Rodríguez-Rosell; Andersen legs ≈ 5–10 RIR vs 1–4 RIR). 2. Absolute-velocity prescription recipe (calibrate one failure set @75%; stop at target m/s not %VL; drift example). 3. Readiness-metric hierarchy: HRV debunked for lifting recovery; VJ with ~1.5 cm cutoff best-validated lower-body readiness gate. 4. "Fixed order + always-keep-a-flex-day" architecture + miss-1/miss-2 rules. 5. Equivalence-testing statistical critique. 6. Peak-intensity-over-average-intensity argument (why RIR advice flips >85% 1RM).

## Caveats / superseded-looking
1. "≥5 RIR maximizes hypertrophy" more aggressive than mainstream "0–3 RIR"; post-2022 work leans back toward closer-to-failure modestly better for hypertrophy at high volumes — treat as exercise- and context-limited (author's own caveats: synergists, short-ROM, isolation). 2. VL thresholds (15–30%) rest almost entirely on Smith-machine squat studies @50–85% 1RM, mostly not volume-equated; author distrusts %VL as prescription tool. 3. Attentional-focus longitudinal effect built on 3 studies. 4. Kilgallon counterexample: failure > 20% VL @85% 1RM — "train far from failure" is intensity- and exercise-bounded. 5. Interference downplaying matches modern view; residual explosive-strength point (SMD −0.28) stands.

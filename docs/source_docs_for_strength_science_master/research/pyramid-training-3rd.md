## Framework (exact)
Six levels, foundational (largest impact) → optional (smallest). ~80% of progress from the bottom four levels; top levels mostly "how not to do it wrong". Layers less clear-cut than Nutrition pyramid because training variables are interdependent (volume/intensity/frequency share one level).
1. L1 Adherence — Realistic, Enjoyable, Flexible ("REF yourself").
2. L2 Volume & Intensity — the training dose: set volume, load, proximity to failure, frequency. Introduces minimum/maximum/practical effective dose, fractional set counting, RPE/RIR.
3. L3 Progression — overload vs progression; training status; fitness-fatigue; deloads/tapers/intro cycles; periodization-as-programming (linearity + variation); Strength Progression System; autoregulated double progression; plateau flowchart.
4. L4 Exercise Selection — movement specificity + variation; PLATE feasibility; exercise order. (For competitive strength athletes this rises; book assumes main lifts included.)
5. L5 Rest Periods — straight-set minimums + time-saving techniques.
6. L6 Lifting Tempo — mostly pitfall-avoidance; technique; mind-muscle connection.

## L1 Adherence
- REF: Realistic (time frame, schedule), Enjoyable, Flexible (when stress is high, energy levels).
- Missed workouts: just resume where you left off; don't cram doubles in hard phases; 3 consecutive training days produce similar gains to spaced sessions.
- One activity must take priority; cardio for lifters: after lifting, or ≥3 h separation, or different days; low-impact/eccentric-light modalities (bike, swim, elliptical, row); 20–30 min sessions or keep under 1 h. Interference: hypertrophy largely unaffected; strength/power can be blunted.
- Injury rates/1000 h: bodybuilding 0.24–1.0, powerlifting 1.0–4.4, weightlifting 2.4–3.3, CrossFit 3.1, strongman 4.5–6.1, Highland Games 7.5 (basketball 8.5–11.1).
- Red flags → professional (radiating pain, bruising, dizziness, nausea); pain is unreliable tissue-damage signal (imaging ≠ symptoms in low-back pain); BFR lets limbs train at 20–30% 1RM during joint pain.

## L2 Volume & Intensity
- Fractional sets: full set for primary muscles/tested lift; half-set for secondary muscles/assistance lifts.
- Strength dose-response: minimum ED 1 set/lift/wk; ~5 sets/lift/wk maxes short-term benefit (set 2 ≈ doubles rate; benefit plateaus ~5th set); practical 3–5 sets/lift/wk short-term, 5–10 sets/muscle/wk long-term (hypertrophy underpins long-term strength). Caveats: studies avg ~10 wks, ~40% untrained, low-volume studies more specific → plateau may be exaggerated for trained lifters.
- Corroboration: powerlifters gain for 6–12 wks on 3–6 heavy sets/lift/wk of 1–5 reps; 1–3 sets plateau ~2 months; 15,000-lifter 1-set dataset plateaued 1–2 years (Steele 2023); Hubal +54% avg curl 1RM in 12 wks on 18 sets/wk; Latella (9,000+ powerlifters): ~10% total increase first competitive year, then ~9 more years for next 10%.
- Practical inverted-U: too much volume → deload spiral, injury, burnout even if research shows no downturn. Volume-load (sets×reps×load) only meaningful within one person/one lift/similar rep ranges. Dose rule: "Do enough to progress, not as much as possible; add volume only when plateaued and everything else is dialed in."
- Reps-allowed table: 100%→1; 95%→2–4; 90%→3–6; 85%→5–9; 80%→8–12; 75%→9–15; 70%→11–18 — variance huge (trained squatters 6–26 reps @70%; Nuzzo 2024) → %1RM for framing/progress-gauging, NOT prescription; prescribe RPE/RIR.
- RIR scale: 10 = 0 RIR; 9.5 = could add load; 9 = 1 RIR; 8.5 = 1–2; 8 = 2; 7.5 = 2–3; 7 = 3; 5–6 = 4–6. RIR accuracy avg ~1-rep underestimate; worse at higher reps (>12) and higher RIR (Halperin 2022). Load correction ~4% per rep off target.
- Failure: momentary failure is the objective definition. RIR's independent effect on strength ≈ negligible; LOAD is the strength driver (linear dose-response: ≥80% > 60–79% > <60%).
- A heavy set must last ≥~4–5 reps to fully count; hypertrophy sets need load >~30% 1RM.
- Strength recommendation: 1–8RM (~80%+ 1RM), ~7–0 RIR as dictated by load; add >90% singles (daily-max data: singles alone small gains; +2×3 @80% of daily max produced meaningful total gains) but most extra volume belongs in hypertrophy work.
- Hypertrophy RIR ladder by exercise class (Guide Table 7.7): lower free-weight compounds 4–8 reps @ 4–2 RIR; lower machine/upper pressing 4–12 @ 4–1 RIR; machine pressing/pulling 6–15 @ 3–0 RIR; isolation 8–20 @ 2–0 RIR. Sequence example: squat/DL 4–6 @3–4 RIR or 6–8 @2–3 RIR; leg press 6–8 @2–3 or 8–12 @1–2; isolation 8–12/12+ @0–2 RIR, final sets to failure optional.
- Frequency: fractional frequency dose-response — biggest jump 1→2 sessions/lift/wk (modeled gain rate ~13%→~17%), diminishing returns to 6/wk. Recommendation 2–6×/lift/wk, 1–2 direct sets per main lift per session. Hypertrophy frequency's independent effect ≈ negligible — a volume-distribution tool: never exceed ~11 fractional sets/muscle/session. Frequency-by-volume table: 4–10 sets → 1–2×; 11–20 → 2–3×; 21–30 → 3+. Skill retention argues ≥2 exposures/lift/wk (one can be light, e.g., 3×1 @80%).

## L3 Progression
- Overload defined by CURRENT adaptation: "it is not what you previously lifted that determines overload, but what you are currently adapted to"; progression confirmed by performance rising.
- Training status expectations: novice add load/reps each session/week (6 months–years); intermediate reps in 10–20 range week-to-week or load month-to-month in low-rep ranges (most reach by year 1, may stay through years 4–5); advanced a rep or two month-to-month or small load increases over longer timeframes. Minimum ED rises with training status; ideal volume doesn't skyrocket.
- Fitness-fatigue: performance = fitness − fatigue. True OTS essentially never demonstrated from lifting alone (Grandou 2020; Fry's 10× daily-1RM × 2 wks the only candidate); underfed-lifter "OTS" = low energy availability.
- Fatigue signs: worse sleep, dreading gym, declining loads/reps, worse aches, more illness. Reactive deload trigger: weekly 5-item self-check (dreading gym / sleep worse / loads or reps decreasing / stress worse / aches worse) — 0–1 yes: continue; 2+: deload.
- Standard deload: cut sets ~30–50% across all exercises, keep load/RIR and frequency; targeted deloads for localized regions (drop deadlifts/squats for lumbar soreness); never deload deeper than needed — cessation >3 days can drop strength.
- Taper: cut volume 50–80%, maintain or slightly raise load; expected +2–4% strength; length scaled to fatigue (days to weeks).
- Intro cycle: start of new higher-volume/lower-intensity phase → run ~75% of planned volume slightly further from failure. Coleman 2024: week 5 completely off in hard 9-wk program gave no benefit, slightly worse strength/soreness → no automatic monthly deloads.
- Plateau volume moves: −20% across the board if chronically under-recovered despite fixes; +20% (global or targeted) only if recovered, technically sound, still stalled.
- Periodization: = long-term planning; programming = short/mid-term. Moesgaard 2022 meta (35 studies, ~1000 participants): periodized > non-periodized for 1RM regardless of model; undulating > for trained lifters; benefits likely from specificity timed near test (linearity) + variation. Zourdos DUP ordering: light singles day between high-volume day and heavy AMRAP day → bigger 1RM gains. Strength programming = dose/frequency + specificity timed right + ordering that protects recovery.
- Strength Progression System: every session opens with a single at prescribed RPE on each main lift (≤5 lifts) → track e1RM trend (same calculator always).
  - Volume phase (6–12 wks): build muscle. 1–3 singles/lift/wk progressing RPE 5→8 (5→2 RIR); secondary lifts 10–20 sets/wk per main-lift muscle group, mostly 6–20 reps @ 0–3 RIR via autoregulated double progression, favoring low-stress non-specific exercises (machines) over high-volume competition lifts. e1RM targets: novice ↑ weekly; intermediate ↑ over phase; advanced maintain.
  - Load phase (4–8 wks): 2–4 singles/lift/wk progressing RPE 6→9 (4→1 RIR); after each single 2 back-off sets of 3–5 reps @ 5–8 RPE (~80–85% of day's e1RM), linearly stepping reps 5→4→3; secondary volume halved to 5–10 sets/wk, −1–2 sets every 1–2 wks. Close variations (SSB, high-bar, block pulls, medium-grip bench) if main-lift volume intolerable. e1RM targets: novice weekly; intermediate every 2–3 wks; advanced over phase.
  - Peak phase (2–4 wks; 2 if volume+load totaled 10–12 wks, 3 if 13–15, 4 if 16+): 2–5 singles/lift/wk at 7–10 RPE; back-off sets shrink 4→2 reps (~85%); secondary 0–4 sets/wk. Final two weeks: w/o −2, sessions ordered SBD ~9–10 RPE → SB ~8–9 → bench-only ~7–8 → SBD opener practice at planned openers (~90–92% 1RM / ~8 RPE), 1×2 back-off after singles; w/o −1: drop bench-only day, singles 7–8 RPE then 6–7 RPE, final SBD 1–2 days out = primer (singles 4–5 RPE only). After peaking: intro week before returning to volume phase.
- Autoregulated double progression (all hypertrophy work + secondary lifts): pick rep+RIR range (e.g., 10–15 @ 0–2 RIR); stop at RIR ceiling; same load for remaining sets; if set 1 misses rep range, correct load ~4% per rep; increase load when set 1 hits rep-range top at RIR-range top; trends over sessions; intermediates ~5 sessions per load jump (novices faster, advanced ~2×); jumping planned increment based on feel is encouraged.
- Plateau decision rules: (1) actually plateaued relative to training status? (2) any "no" on: sleeping 7+ h; not in energy deficit; protein ≥0.7 g/lb (1.6 g/kg); neither over- nor under-rating RPE; muscles/lifts trained ≥2×/wk (strength); exercise selection/ROM/tempo appropriate and consistent → fix it, rerun a mesocycle. (3) Recovering? (0–1 "yes" on fatigue checklist → likely recovering; 2+ → not). Not recovering → light week; if fatigue returns quickly, permanently cut volume ~20% or reorganize. Recovering + stalled → increase dose ~20% or fix technique. Joint pain only → switch to 12–20-rep sets, or BFR limbs at 20–30% 1RM @ 0–3 RIR (wrap ~7/10 tightness).

## L4 Exercise Selection
- Specificity multidimensional (movement × load), not a simple spectrum: complex lifts are specificity-sensitive and transfer outward (squat → leg press) but simple lifts don't transfer inward (Rossi 2018).
- ~5 fractional sets/lift/wk captures maximal benefit of specific training; extra specific heavy volume has opportunity cost (González-Badillo 2006: 46 vs 93 vs 184 heavy reps @90–100% over 10 wks — no significant difference; moderate best) — invest extra volume in hypertrophy-support work.
- Varied practice (contextual interference, Fonseca 2014) can beat single-exercise training, especially early/intermediate; variation must stay relevant.
- Targeted secondary lifts (Helms coaching anecdotes): sumo swap for recoverability; heavy rack holds for grip; minimal-squat + machine volume for long-femur lifter; paused deadlifts at mid-shin for bar drift.
- Sticking points: pausing at the VISIBLE sticking point is logically flawed (braking ≠ force; visible stall follows the force deficit) but pauses help control/position; isometrics at force deficits plausible but hard to locate without lab tools; variations that punish technical faults (front squat for squat-morning); accommodating resistance (bands/chains): no average benefit (2022 meta ~500 participants) but possibly useful near lockout or equipped lifters; explosive/speed work individually responsive.
- PLATE feasibility: Painful? Loadable? Available? Time-efficient? Enjoyable?
- Exercise order: strength gains slightly greater when exercise performed first (2021 meta, 11 studies); hypertrophy indifferent; multi-joint free weights first; weak-point exception only if it won't hurt big lifts.

## L5 Rest Periods
- Strength main lifts: rest until ready, ≥2 min minimum (Grgic 2018 review), often 3–5+; acceptable set-to-set drift ~1–2 reps/RIR.
- Hypertrophy: ≥90 s average; more for strong lifters/lower-body compounds.
- Supersets: antagonist-paired and peripheral-paired preserve (sometimes beat) straight-set performance; 2024 RCTs: same hypertrophy in half to two-thirds the time at ~+1 RPE cost; rest ~2 min between supersets (compound pairs 3–5-min cycle; isolation 1–2 min); never pair lower-body compounds with other big lifts.
- Drop sets: load −~20% per drop; ~13 drop sets ≈ 10 straight sets for hypertrophy (Sødal 2023); counting rule: 4 drop sets (incl. initial) = 3 straight sets; biggest time savings.
- Rest-pause: 20-s mini-rests, same load, until target reps (e.g., 30 total); similar adaptations volume-matched (Enes 2021 — rest-pause group gained most strength by doing most reps at heaviest load); impractical for heavy squats; more fatigue than straight sets.
- Longo 2022: rest structure matters only insofar as it changes total volume.

## L6 Tempo
- Muscles ~40% stronger eccentrically. Strength: lower as fast as control allows; lift concentrically with maximal intent; intentionally slow concentrics impair strength gains (Hermes & Fry 2023, 625 participants).
- Hypertrophy: eccentric 1–4 s under muscular control; never let gravity move the load; total rep duration ≤8 s (Schoenfeld 2015; Moreno-Villanueva 2022: >8 s rep duration = poorer hypertrophy); slow-eccentric 3–6 s makes light loads more stimulative when pain/fatigue limits heavy work.
- Pauses: dissipate elastic energy (acutely weaker) but pin/pause training can build active force in sticking region (pin squats ↑ 1RM same as back squats); 1–2 s stretch pauses won't meaningfully boost hypertrophy.
- Supramaximal eccentrics: small non-significant lower-body 1RM edge (Buskard 2018), higher damage/injury risk — not advised without specialist setup.
- Mind-muscle connection: useful only for light/isolation work; internal focus can inhibit heavy-lift strength — use external cues.
- Technique: no universal "correct" form; meet sport's standards, maximize 1RM, stay pain-free and consistent (consistent tempo/ROM so progress is real).

## Guide to Program Building
- Days/wk first: 2 minimum; most need 3–5; advanced rarely on 2. Strength Frequency Matrix (Table 7.1): days/wk (2–6) → main-lift weekly frequencies (1.5–6), with combined S/B and B/D sessions; assistance sharing musculature adds 0.5 to a lift's frequency.
- ~90% of lifters: 3–5 days/wk, muscle/lift frequency 2–4×/wk.
- Volume tiers by time budget (hypertrophy): 4–8 sets/muscle (~1–2.5 h/wk) lowest; 9–12 (~3–4.5 h); 13–16 (~5–6.5 h); 17–20 (~7–8.5 h); 21–30 (~9+ h, specialization) highest if sustainable.
- Avoid heavy secondary work for a muscle the day before its main-lift work.
- Main-lift load/RPE: no cheat sheet — dictated by phase and proximity to peak.

## Warm-up
- Purposes: temperature, tissue/neuromuscular prep, small injury-risk reduction. Static stretching alone: no independent injury-prevention effect (2025 meta, 15 trials, >9,000 participants); pre-training static can blunt strength/power; brief (<60 s) not-to-discomfort static harmless; static-then-dynamic ordering preserves/increases performance; foam rolling ~60 s modest pressure slightly helps.
- Full-ROM lifting ≈ stretching for ROM (2023 meta, 55 studies, 2,756 participants) — warm-up sets with gradually increasing ROM usually suffice.
- Template: optional 5-min general aerobic; 3–5-min dynamic drills (7–10 min if no general warm-up); ascending warm-up sets (1–2-min rests): for ≤6-rep working sets — 5–10 reps ≤40%, 3–5 @~60%, 1–3 @~80%, 1 @90%; for ≥6-rep sets — 5–10 @40%, 4–6 @60%, 2–4 @80%, 1–2 @85–90% (optional PAPE using %1RM of the day).
- PAPE: 1–2 sets of 1–3 reps @85–90% 1RM 5–15 min before working sets typically adds ~1–2 reps to failure (range 1–7 when present); the system's daily singles double as PAPE. Machines need no warm-up beyond first exercise for a muscle.

## Caveats / conflicts with mainstream
1. Anti-%1RM prescribing (RPE-first) — defensible/current but diverges from classic percentage-based powerlifting programming; still uses ~80–85% anchors for back-offs.
2. Deload skepticism — argues against calendar deloads (Coleman 2024), "hidden fatigue" narrative, OTS-from-lifting — stronger pushback than much mainstream writing.
3. Strength-volume plateau ~5 sets/lift — rests on a preprint meta-regression (authors caveat: short studies, untrained majority, specificity confounds); most contestable quantitative claim.
4. Frequency benefit to 6×/lift — same preprint; "1→2 is the big jump" is the safe takeaway.
5. "RIR negligible for strength" true only load-controlled; hasty reading risk.
6. Hypertrophy ceiling ~30 sets conservative; taper/deload percentages expert-derived, not trial-derived.
7. Debunked legacy ideas: GH-from-short-rests, TUT as growth driver, slow concentrics for strength, pause-at-visible-stall, mandatory stretching warm-up, lifting-induced overtraining, supramaximal eccentrics as clear winner.

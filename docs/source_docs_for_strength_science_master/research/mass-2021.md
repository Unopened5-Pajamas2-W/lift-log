## Claims (atomic)

**Proximity to failure (Zourdos on Santanielo et al. 2020)**
- Study: 14 trained men, 10 wks, within-subjects: unilateral leg press + leg extension 2×/wk; one leg to failure @75% 1RM, other stopped by perceived fatigue (~1.5–1.6 RIR). 1RM re-tested wk5; loads reset to 75% of new 1RM. Set prescription = habitual weekly quad sets +20%, split 50/50 → 11.5±5.1 sets/wk leg press, 11.6±5.2 leg extension (range 4–25). [§Methods]
- Failure leg did 13.6% more reps/set (12.0±2.1 vs 10.4±2.8) and 11.5% more volume. [§Findings]
- Outcome: all measures improved in both legs, no significant differences; quad CSA +13.5% (failure) vs +18.1% (non-failure) → 4.6% faster growth ES 0.27; leg-press 1RM +4.4% faster non-failure ES 0.18 (trivial after correction); pennation angle ES 0.46 favoring non-failure. [§Findings]
- Core rule: 1–2 RIR shy of failure at least as good for hypertrophy, likely better for strength. Trained lifters on high-frequency programs: most compound sets ~4–5 RIR (possibly farther) still maximize hypertrophy; if hitting a muscle 3×/week or high volume, 3–5 RIR on main lifts mitigates carryover fatigue. 5 RIR is a floor estimate, not ceiling claim. [§Interpretation/Application]
- RIR stratification across week (Mon/Wed/Fri example): early-week/high-volume — main lifts and "damaging" exercises (flys, RDLs, skull crushers) farther from failure; assistance (curls, lateral raises, leg extensions) 1–2 RIR, last few sets to failure acceptable. Late week — last set of main lift to failure, all assistance 0–2 RIR. Not all-or-none; lifter buy-in/enjoyment justifies strategic failure work. [§Application]
- RIR accuracy (Zourdos 2019 acute): one set to failure @70% 1RM squat; lifters predicting 5/3/1 RIR under by ~5/3.5/2 reps (actual ~10/~6.5/3 RIR). Intra-set RIR predictable to within 1 rep after first set (Hackett; Mansfield). Helms thesis: 8 wks squat+bench 3×/wk, 4–5 RIR ≈ 2–3 RIR for growth. [§Interpretation]
- Strength loading: maximizing 1RM requires working up to ≥90% 1RM; a single @90% is already ~2 RIR; competition prep may include heavy singles at 0–1 RIR (RPE 9–9.5). Avoid failure during volume work; intensity work naturally approaches failure; both volume and intensity drive strength. [§Application]
- Velocity-loss proxies (strongest strength evidence cited): Pareja-Blanco — stopping squat sets at 20% VL → +4.6% greater squat 1RM vs 40% VL (≈ failure on >50% of sets), 8 wks. Sánchez-Moreno — pull-ups 25% vs 50% VL → ~5% greater strength gains with 25%, 8 wks. [§Findings]
- Counter-evidence weighed and discounted: Goto 2005 — leg extensions 5×10 to failure (+12.9% quad) vs 30-s intra-set rest after 5th rep (+4.0%), 12 wks — discounted as extended-cluster artifact. Karsten — 4×10RM ~5% faster quad growth than 8×5 @10RM load (≈5 RIR→3 RIR), but once-weekly muscle frequency lowers weight. [§Interpretation]
- Frequency: 2–3×/week superior to 1×/week for strength (Grgic 2018 meta); possibly preferable for hypertrophy (Schoenfeld 2016/2019 metas). [§Application]
- Proposed future design: 4 groups at failure / 1–3 RIR / 4–6 RIR / 7–10 RIR with damage markers to 48h, session RPE, 12-wk adherence follow-up. [§Next Steps]

**Concurrent training / interference (Zourdos on Kilen et al. 2020)**
- Study: 290 Danish military conscripts (285 M/6 F; 87-person biopsy subsample), 9 wks, 2 h/wk study training on top of ~20 h/wk military marching/standing. Groups: micro (eight 15-min sessions/wk — 4 running + 4 lifting, ≥2 h apart, ≤3/day); classic (30 min running then 30 min lifting, 2×/wk); standard (two 60-min mixed sessions/wk ~40% lifting). Running progressed moderate → intervals. Lifting volume = 6 sets/week × 5 reps (minimal). [§Methods]
- Outcome: pull-ups rose in micro & classic (both p<0.05 vs standard); knee-extensor MVC rose in all (no group differences); only micro improved elbow-flexor strength; grip unchanged. Biopsy: type II fiber area +12.81% (combined); satellite cells per type II fiber +33.33% (combined +22.22%); micro type IIa proportion +5.1%; combined type I distribution −3.7%. [§Findings]
- Interference rules: dose- and mode-dependent — studies that show it are designed to cause it (running 1 h/day 4–6 d/wk = pronounced; cycling 3×30 min = negligible). Lower-body lifting volume drops ~25% when lifting 4 h after aerobic work, still impaired at 8 h (Sporer & Wenger 2003). Running interferes more than cycling (Wilson 2012 meta). Interference "all but disappears when a caloric deficit is avoided" (Murach & Bagley). Hickson 1980 precedent: −0.8 kg body mass yet +25% squat 1RM in 10 wks. [§Interpretation]
- Practical protocol: if cardio and lifting adjacent, lift first. 15-minute lifting micro-sessions spread through week keep main lifts away from cardio; squats/deadlifts on non-cardio days or immediately before aerobic work; only assistance work after cardio. [§Application]
- Cardio mode: HIIT/sprinting mechanistically lifting-like but sprint muscle damage lasts 48–72h; a meta (Sabag 2018) shows HIIT can attenuate strength gains; cycling sprints beat running sprints. Steady-state cycling 30 min @50–60% VO2max, 2×/wk → no measurable negative effect on strength/hypertrophy (Dolan 2015). Flexible template: 12 cardio sessions/month, 6 HIIT + 6 non-running mode, sprints on high-energy days. Running 1–2×/wk, a few miles, fine if not immediately before lifting or ~24 h before a heavy lower-body day. [§Application]

## Evidence quality
Strong: named/dated studies with journals. Notable: Davies 2016 (failure meta w/ erratum acknowledged); Grgic 2018 (frequency-strength meta); Wilson 2012 (interference meta); Sabag 2018 (HIIT-concurrent meta); Schoenfeld 2016/2019 (frequency metas). Reviewed studies 2020; supporting base 2016–2020 + classics. Self-flagged limits: small samples, no equivalence testing, no lifting-only control in Kilen, self-reported RIR.

## Unique content
1. "How far from failure can you train?" reframing; 4–5 RIR compounds on 3×/wk sufficiency claim. 2. Zourdos' intra-set RIR-prediction error data (5/3/1 misjudged by ~5/3.5/2 reps). 3. Week-structured RIR placement by exercise damage profile. 4. 15-minute lifting micro-sessions as cardio-interference workaround. 5. Velocity-loss thresholds (20/25%) as precise non-failure stopping rules. 6. Satellite-cell/fiber-type data from concurrent trial. 7. Statistics primer on equivalence testing (Lakens).

## Caveats / superseded-looking
1. 5-RIR sufficiency claim leans ahead of later proximity-to-failure metas (2022–2024) which shift back toward ≤3 RIR (esp. hypertrophy); treat as directional, not established optimality; article itself concedes data can't settle 2–3 vs 4–5 RIR. 2. "Failure might be inferior for strength" rests on small short velocity-loss trials; directionally defensible, overconfident as written. 3. "2–3×/wk may be preferable for hypertrophy" later neutralized by volume-equated analyses. 4. "Interference disappears when deficit avoided" — single narrative-review claim, overstated. 5. Internal inconsistency: "eight weeks" vs 10-wk study. 6. Loose "5 RPE = 5 RIR" usage.

## Claims

### Proximity to failure & recovery time course (Pareja-Blanco 2018, reviewed by Zourdos)
- Study: 10 trained men (2–4 yrs), 20 weeks, 10 conditions; each session = 3 sets Smith bench + squat; fatigue at pre, 6h, 24h, 48h.
- Failure protocols (reps done (reps possible)): 12(12), 10(10), 8(8), 6(6), 4(4). Non-failure: 6(12), 5(10), 4(8), 3(6), 2(4) — proximity range 5RPE–8RPE.
- Load-% mapping: 12 reps ≈70%, 10 ≈75%, 8 ≈80%, 6 ≈85%, 4 ≈90% 1RM.
- Decision rules: every failure protocol dropped vertical jump significantly at 24h (−4.4% to −7.2%); zero non-failure protocols did. At 48h, failure protocols with ≥6 reps still ~−4% VJ; non-failure <−0.7%. CK still elevated at 48h in all failure conditions; highest in 12(12)/10(10)/8(8).
- Volume-equation example: 5(10) vs 4(4) ≈ equal tonnage (1,050 vs 1,080 kg on a 100-kg squatter), yet failure still lengthened recovery — failure's fatigue cost is not just volume.
- Cross-study anchor: Watkins 2017 — an 8% VJ drop predicted a 28% drop in squat reps at 80% 1RM (r = 0.65), i.e., >2 lost reps/set.
- Programming rule: "12 sets/week squat over 3 sessions" example — 5 sets to failure at 70% (15,10,7,6,5 reps) blocks retraining until ~Friday and breaks the 3× frequency; 4×8 at RPE 5–8 (2–5 RIR) permits Wed/Fri sessions. Weekly volume/frequency beats per-session volume.
- Proximity guidance: stay further from failure in volume/accumulation blocks; closer to failure in intensity/peaking blocks.
- Don't train habitually at 5RPE — leaves strength on the table (Helms dissertation: 5–6 RPE 3×/wk × 8 wks gave similar hypertrophy to ~8 RPE in well-trained lifters).
- Failure acceptable for: single-joint assistance work; the last session before your longest break (e.g., Friday before 72h rest); only the final set of a main lift; keep technique tight at 10RPE (breakdown is a known cost).
- Failure ≠ more strength (Davies 2016 meta).
- Evidence: RCT (Pareja-Blanco 2018, JSCR); supporting: Davies et al. 2016 (Sports Med) failure meta; Morán-Navarro 2017 (EJAP); Helms et al. 2018 (Front Physiol); Watkins 2017. Limits flagged: n=10–14, non-failure conditions confounded by differing rep counts, no velocity monitoring.

### Individualized RFD response: strength vs power (Peltonen 2018, reviewed by Zourdos)
- Study: 24 untrained men; 14 trained 2×/wk × 20 weeks (leg press/extension/curl) — weeks 1–10 strength block: 2–4 sets × 6–10 reps @ 70–80% 1RM; weeks 11–20 power block: 3–5 sets × 4–10 reps @ 30–50% 1RM. Isometric leg-extension RFD tested pre/post every 7th session; 10 controls.
- Findings: 6 max-strength responders, 4 power responders, 4 non-responders. Max-strength responders: +100% RFD in strength block, +3% after power block. Power responders: +11% in strength block, +53% after power block. Hypertrophy: +12% both responder types, ~0 non-responders; leg-press 1RM similar across groups (+12.38% / +15.17% / +16.11%).
- Decision rules: power responder heuristic — keep a 2:1 ratio of normal strength work (≥70% 1RM, multiple reps, ≥5 RPE) to power work; in 3×/wk DUP, mid-week session power-focused; in block periodization, 1 power block per 3.
- Alternative power formats: dynamic effort 40–60% 1RM × 2–3 reps/set, or high-intensity singles @80–90% 1RM; stop sets before bar velocity slows.
- Under-recovery signal: consistently short of failure, only 2–5 sets/exercise, high frequency, yet chronically under-recovered → swap one session for power work; power sessions double as taper/deload.
- Non-responders: hypertrophy-resistant but strength still gains — prioritize frequent-ish heavy singles on main lifts at 7–8 RPE every few weeks (possibly weekly), low volume/high intensity, neural emphasis.
- Context: Hubal 2005 — 0–250% strength-gain range in 12 weeks.
- Evidence: RCT (Peltonen 2018, EJAP). Responder taxonomy built on tiny samples — directional, not diagnostic.

## Corpus-internal flags

- "Failure training gives no extra strength": mainstream position in corpus; MASS-20/21/22 later refine with load-dependence nuance.
- "5–6 RPE ≈ 8 RPE for hypertrophy": Helms dissertation, small sample; treated as lower bound, not prescription (article itself cautions against habitual 5RPE).
- Responder typologies: tiny samples; directional only.
- Power block at 30–50% 1RM atypical; article itself acknowledges better-specified formats (40–60% dynamic effort, 80–90% singles).
- VJ as readiness proxy rests on Watkins 2017.
- CK as fatigue indicator: weak proxy.

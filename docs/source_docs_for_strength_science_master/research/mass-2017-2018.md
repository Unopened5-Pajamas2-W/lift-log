## Claims

### Volume → strength gains (Nuckols on Ralston 2017)
- Volume bins: <5 sets/exercise/week (LWS); 5–9 (MWS); ≥10 (HWS); analyses pooled to <5 vs ≥5.
- Meta: 9 studies (4 trained, 5 untrained), 8–26 weeks, mean 2.8±0.3 sessions/wk, mean 78.2±4.1% 1RM, weekly volumes 1–12 sets.
- ≥5 sets/exercise/week significantly better (p=0.003), but effect sizes trivial–small: ΔES 0.18 overall; 0.18 multi-joint; 0.23 single-joint; 0.14 exercise-specific 1RM (p=0.06).
- Nuckols' reframe: trivial ES can be practically big — ~20–25% faster strength gains with higher volume.
- Comparison with Schoenfeld 2017 hypertrophy meta: 10+ sets ES 0.52 vs <5 sets 0.31 (Δ0.21; Δ0.10 after outlier removal) — absolute volume advantage similar for strength and hypertrophy; relative advantage larger for hypertrophy (33–60%).
- Decision rules: low volume still yields solid short-term gains (fine for busy periods/non-competitors); higher volume matters more as training age rises; data stop at 10 sets/exercise/week — the tipping point where strength gains degrade is unknown; use as rough start point, adjust to recovery/response.
- Evidence: MA (Ralston et al. 2017, Sports Med) + Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017. Also cites Krieger 2009, Morton 2017 (protein), Hales 2009.

### Squat vs deadlift fatigue (Zourdos on Barnes 2017)
- Protocol: 10 trained men (≥2 yrs, 3×/wk; squat 1RM 158 kg, DL 191 kg); crossover 8×2 @95% 1RM, 5-min rest, 1 week apart; measures pre, 5 min, 30 min post.
- Result: no significant between-lift differences in MVIC, EMG, voluntary activation, control stimulus force, or T/C — deadlift does not cause greater acute central/peripheral fatigue than squat.
- Limits (stated in article): high-intensity only (no high-volume test); no 24–96 h recovery time course; only quads measured (posterior chain untested) — weekly total-body deadlift fatigue may still be higher.
- Frequency: squat 3×/wk vs deadlift 1×/wk yielded +9.21% vs +7.14% 1RM over 8 weeks (Zourdos DUP study) — lower deadlift frequency is viable, not mandatory; frequency can be raised gradually via repeated bout effect; macro periodization can be identical across lifts while weekly frequency/volume differ.
- Frequency should be set by individual biomechanics, injury history, training age, availability, preference. "The best way to improve the deadlift is to deadlift" (Hales 2009) endorsed; some squat→DL crossover exists.
- Why long-term deadlift research is scarce: novelty effect inflates gains, injury risk, time cost (candid meta-scientific aside).
- Evidence: RCT (Barnes et al. 2017).

### Velocity-based training (Zourdos on Banyard 2017)
- 18 men (squat ≥1.5×BM; mean 142 kg); standard 1RM warm-up: 3 reps @20/40/60% + 1 @80/90%.
- Reliability rule: average concentric velocity (ACV) & mean propulsive velocity reliable at 20–90% 1RM, NOT at 100% (CV% 19.4%/18.0% at 1RM); peak velocity reliable everywhere. Acceptance criteria: ICC >0.70, CV <10%, ES <0.59.
- Equivalent prescriptions (squat): 4×8 @70% = 4×8 @ RPE 5–7 = 4×8 @ 0.40–0.70 m/s; 3×1 @80% = RPE <5 = 0.55–0.75 m/s; 5×3 @85% = RPE 7–9 = 0.30–0.45 m/s (velocity = last rep of set).
- Anchors: 1RM @90% ≈ ACV 0.34 m/s ≈ RPE 8–9 → heavy singles: 3×1 @ 0.30–0.40 m/s. Cross-study: ACV at 100% 1RM ≈ 0.23–0.26 m/s.
- Cooke thesis ACV/RPE ladder (squat, n=58, unpublished master's thesis — weakest link): 30%→1.06 m/s (RPE ~1.5), 40%→1.00 (2), 50%→0.91 (3), 60%→0.82 (4), 70%→0.69 (5), 80%→0.55 (6.5), 90%→0.39 (8.5), 100%→0.26 (9.5).
- Warm-up readiness rule (60% ACV; smallest worthwhile change 0.06 m/s): <0.76 m/s & RPE >4 → light day; 0.76–0.88 & RPE 3–4 → moderate; >0.88 & RPE <3 → heavy day; usable for attempt selection on 1RM days.
- Progress without maxing: same load moved faster = stronger (200 kg single 0.35→0.45 m/s); velocity gauges block-to-block progress/taper.
- VBT + RPE complementary: velocity ignores technique error, RPE/RIR captures it; warm-up RPE @85% may predict daily 1RM better than velocity (3-person case series only).
- Femur length does NOT predict squat ACV (r=0.02; confirmed in Cooke); training age does (longer-trained lift slower 1RM velocities).
- Helms dissertation: RPE-autoregulated 8-wk prescription beat %1RM prescription for squat/bench gains.
- Evidence: reliability study (Banyard, Nosaka, Vernon & Haff 2017) + dissertation/case-series caveats as flagged.
